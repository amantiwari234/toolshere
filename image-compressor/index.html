About Image Compressor & Resizer

ToolsHere Image Compressor & Resizer is a client-side utility built for students, job applicants, web developers, and designers who need quick, precise image optimization. Whether you must meet strict government portal upload limits—such as 20KB, 50KB, or 100KB requirements for exams, passports, and job applications—or reduce image payload sizes for faster web performance, this tool gives you complete manual and preset control. You can adjust exact pixel dimensions, dial in quality percentages from 10% to 100%, and inspect instant side-by-side file size feedback. Everything processes entirely within your device's browser memory using HTML5 Canvas, ensuring your personal photos never leave your computer or phone.

How to Use Image Compressor & Resizer (Step-by-Step)

  1. 1 Click or drag your image file (PNG, JPG, WebP, or GIF) into the upload dropzone.
  2. 2 Choose a quick preset button (20KB, 50KB, 100KB, 200KB) or drag the compression slider to set your custom quality level.
  3. 3 Optionally adjust the pixel width and height to resize the dimensions to exact specifications.
  4. 4 Review the live compressed file size and visual preview on the screen.
  5. 5 Click 'Download Compressed Image' to save your optimized JPG file instantly.

Frequently Asked Questions (Image Compressor & Resizer)

How does the target size preset (20KB, 50KB, 100KB) work?

The tool automatically calibrates the image compression ratio and resolution algorithms in browser memory to aim for your selected target threshold, making it easy to comply with government form restrictions.

Are my photos uploaded to any external server?

No. The entire compression and resizing pipeline runs inside your browser via the HTML5 Canvas API. Your pictures never leave your local device.

What output format will my compressed image be saved in?

The compressor exports as a standard JPEG (.jpg) file, which provides the best balance of file size reduction and image clarity for online portals.

Can I resize the width and height without losing quality?

Yes. You can enter specific pixel values into the width and height input fields. Downscaling dimensions also dramatically reduces the final file weight.
